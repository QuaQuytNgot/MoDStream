01 / The problem

A shared link.
Unequal visual importance.

A dynamic scene is more than a single stream.

Each independently encoded 3D Gaussian object has its own quality choices, but all objects compete for the same bandwidth and must share a playback timeline.

Being inside the viewport does not guarantee being visible. An object behind another may contribute little to the final image. MoDGStream combines what reaches the viewport with how much an upgrade reduces distortion to coordinate object quality.

Joint visibility×Distortion gain→Quality selection

Before streaming

Four quality levels.
Measured upgrade benefits.

StreamSTGS prepares four standalone representations per object and GOP. Encoded resources and a manifest describe the available choices; offline MSE profiles describe their distortion.

The client combines these profiles with joint-scene visibility and an estimated bandwidth budget.

How to read these maps +

Joint contribution sums each object’s Gaussian alpha weighted by transmittance. Solo contribution renders the same object without the others. Grayscale panels share a linear 0–1 scale, without individual contrast enhancement. The color mix is an additive combination of the measured joint maps, not a segmentation mask.

Occlusion loss = 1 − joint area / solo area. The displayed weight is joint area / sum of joint areas across these three objects. These are single-sample weights, not a replayed adaptation decision. Measurements use the full 1920 × 1440 viewport; every image uses the same display crop. Runtime analysis at a lower resolution can give different weights.

Evaluation protocol and selected data +

HTTP/2 delivery uses one persistent connection, up to 16 concurrent streams, a 2-second startup buffer, and a 6-second buffer ceiling. Visual quality is evaluated offline against the jointly rendered all-CRF20 reference at matching camera poses. Reported rebuffering measures download-side continuity and excludes end-to-end decoding and rendering latency.

MSE-based selection does not consistently outperform V-P. Under HV with 20 objects in the linear layout, the paper reports 22.77 dB for Ours and 23.44 dB for V-P. At 20 objects under LS, none of the compared methods sustains uninterrupted 20-second playback. Missing exhaustive-search runs are unavailable, not zero.

The paper

Abstract

Virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) applications increasingly rely on volumetric video to provide immersive six-degree-of-freedom (6DoF) experiences. Recent advances in dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enable high-quality rendering of time-varying scenes, making them promising for interactive volumetric applications. However, streaming multiple independently encoded dynamic 3DGS objects over bandwidth-constrained networks remains challenging. Objects share limited network resources but contribute unequally to the user’s viewport due to their spatial relationships, occlusion, and representation quality.

In this paper, we present MoDGStream, a visibility-aware adaptive streaming framework for multiple dynamic 3DGS objects. MoDGStream estimates each object’s visual importance through joint Gaussian alpha compositing and combines the resulting visibility weights with offline mean squared error (MSE) profiles to estimate the benefit of quality upgrades. We formulate joint object-level quality selection as a bandwidth-constrained optimization problem and develop a discretized dynamic-programming algorithm for efficient GOP-level adaptation. We evaluate MoDGStream using HTTP/2 delivery under four Lumos5G network traces, with up to 20 objects and different spatial arrangements. Experimental results show improved viewport quality over baseline adaptation in many bandwidth-feasible scenarios.
